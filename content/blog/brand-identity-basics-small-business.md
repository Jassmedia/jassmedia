A brand identity is not just a logo. It is the set of visual and verbal choices that make your business recognisable everywhere it appears: your signboard, Instagram page, website, packaging and invoices. When those choices are consistent, people remember you and trust you faster.

You do not need a 100-page brand book. This is what a small business actually needs.

## 1. A logo in every version you will use

One logo file is not enough. Ask for:

- **Primary logo:** the full version, for your website header and signboard.
- **Icon or mark:** a compact version for profile pictures, favicons and small spaces.
- **Light and dark versions:** so the logo works on white and on dark backgrounds.
- **File types:** a vector file (SVG, AI or PDF) that can be enlarged without blurring, plus PNGs with transparent backgrounds.

The vector file is the one most businesses lose. Keep it safe; every printer and designer will ask for it.

## 2. A small colour palette

Pick one or two main brand colours and one or two neutrals. Write down their exact codes (HEX for screens, CMYK for print) so every designer uses the same shade. Fewer colours, used consistently, look more professional than many.

## 3. Two fonts

Choose one font for headings and one for body text, both easy to read on a phone. Note where to download them and which weights to use. Using the same fonts on your website, posts and printed material is one of the quickest ways to look consistent.

## 4. A tone of voice

How does your brand sound? Friendly or formal, playful or expert, Hindi-English mix or English only? Write three or four short rules, with examples of what to say and what to avoid. This keeps captions, website copy and WhatsApp replies feeling like the same business.

## 5. A photography and imagery style

Decide whether you use real photos of your team and work, product shots on plain backgrounds, illustrations, or a mix. Real photos almost always build more trust than stock images. A consistent style, with similar lighting and colours, makes your feed and website feel cohesive.

## 6. Ready-made templates

Templates save time and stop your brand drifting. The most useful ones are:

- social media post and story templates
- a quotation and invoice layout
- a visiting card and letterhead
- a simple presentation deck

## 7. A one-page brand guide

Put everything on one or two pages: logo versions and how not to use them, colour codes, fonts, tone of voice rules and a few example layouts. Share it with anyone who creates anything for your business.

## Signs your brand needs attention

- Your logo looks different on your board, website and Instagram.
- You do not have a sharp, high-resolution logo file to send a printer.
- Every post uses different colours and fonts.
- Customers mention your competitor's name more easily than yours.

If any of these sound familiar, a short branding project usually pays for itself in how much more established you look.
