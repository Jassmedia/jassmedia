Plenty of business websites look good and still bring in almost no enquiries. Visitors arrive, scroll for a few seconds, and leave without calling. In most cases the problem is not the design. The site simply does not make it obvious what you do, why someone should trust you, and how to get in touch.

These are the eight things we check first on every website we build or fix.

## 1. Say what you do in the first screen

A visitor should understand within five seconds who you are, what you offer and who it is for. "Interior design for 2 and 3 BHK homes in Hyderabad" beats "Crafting spaces that inspire". Put that line at the top, in plain words, with one button underneath.

## 2. Make the next step impossible to miss

Decide what you want visitors to do (call, send a WhatsApp message, book a visit, request a quote) and make that the main button everywhere. Keep a WhatsApp or call button visible on phones at all times. If people have to search for your contact details, many will not bother.

## 3. Load fast on a phone

Most visitors arrive on a phone, often on mobile data. A site that takes more than three seconds to load loses a large share of them before they see anything. Compress images, avoid heavy sliders and autoplay videos, and test on a real mid-range phone, not just your office computer.

## 4. Show proof, not just promises

People trust other customers more than they trust your claims. Add real reviews with names, photos of completed work, before-and-afters, the logos of businesses you have worked with, and any numbers you can honestly stand behind. Place proof near your buttons, where people are deciding.

## 5. Give each service its own section or page

A single block of text listing everything you do forces visitors to work out whether you can help them. Give each service a clear heading, a short explanation of what is included, who it suits and what happens next. It also helps visitors who arrive looking for one specific thing.

## 6. Keep forms short

Every extra field on a form costs enquiries. For a first contact, name, phone number and one question about what they need is usually enough. You can gather the rest on the call.

## 7. Look consistent with your brand

Your website should feel like the same business as your Instagram page, your shop board and your visiting card: the same logo, colours, tone and photography style. Consistency makes a business look established and trustworthy, especially to someone who has just discovered you.

## 8. Track what visitors do

Set up analytics so you can see which pages people visit, where they drop off, and how many click to call, WhatsApp or submit a form. Without this you are guessing. With it, you can make one change at a time and see whether enquiries go up.

## A quick self-check

Open your website on your phone and ask:

- Can I tell what this business does within five seconds?
- Can I contact them with one tap from any page?
- Did the page load quickly?
- Can I see proof that real customers are happy?

If any answer is no, start there. Small fixes in these areas usually do more for enquiries than a complete redesign.
